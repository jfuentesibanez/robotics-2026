# Illustration prompts — GPT Images 2.5

17 illustrations for *A Look at the Present and Future of Robotics*.

**How to use them**
1. Generate `01-golem` **first**. It sets the look. Keep that image in the conversation and
   attach it as a style reference for every prompt after it — say *"same illustrator, same
   palette, same line weight as the attached image"*. This is what stops the set drifting.
2. Save each file under the name given in its heading, into the `img/` folder next to
   `robotics-2026.html`. The deck picks them up automatically and drops the placeholder drawings.
3. Square (1:1). The deck crops nothing.

## STYLE BLOCK — paste this ahead of every subject line

> Editorial illustration. Hand-drawn ink line work with loose watercolour washes, in the
> tradition of classic European press and comic illustration — visible pencil under the ink,
> slightly uneven line weight, expressive and lightly exaggerated proportions. The washes sit
> a few millimetres off the ink lines rather than filling them exactly, the way real watercolour
> does. Warm limited palette: sepia, ochre, brass, rust, with one single cold accent per image.
> A steampunk register throughout — riveted plates, brass, exposed springs and pin joints,
> nineteenth-century workshop dress. Flat plain background in ivory #F7F4EE, edge to edge, no
> gradient, no vignette, no cast shadow, no ground plane, no frame or border. One subject,
> grounded by at most two props, nothing else in the scene. Absolutely no text, letters,
> numerals, runes, logos or watermarks anywhere in the image. No real person's face and no
> existing character or branded product design. Square format, print quality.

## 01-golem.png
Cover, and *The Golem* (slide 6)

A massive humanoid figure of grey-brown clay standing completely still, shoulders hunched
forward, heavy arms hanging at its sides, hands like blocks. Crude unfinished face with deep-set
shadowed eye sockets and no expression; a single shallow incised mark on the forehead, purely
geometric, not a letter. Thumb-pressed ridges still visible in the clay of the chest and
forearms. Cold accent: a faint blue-grey in the eye sockets. Mood: patient, inert, waiting to be
told.

## 02-talos.png
*Talos* (slide 4)

A colossal bronze automaton mid-stride, one arm raised overhead holding a jagged boulder ready
to throw. Archaic Greek plate armour over the torso and thighs, rivets and seams visible along
every plate edge, verdigris creeping into the joints. Blank staring face with no pupils. One
small bronze plug set into the ankle, clearly a weak point. Cold accent: the boulder. Mood:
implacable.

## 03-pandora.png
*Pandora* (slide 5)

A standing female figure who is plainly a made object rather than a person: her left arm and
shoulder are still sectioned like a jointed wooden doll, with a visible assembly seam running
down that side of her body, the rest finished and smooth. Draped classical robe. Beside her a
large ceramic storage jar, waist-high, its lid leaning against the base. Her expression is calm
and unreadable. Cold accent: the jar's glazed mouth. Mood: beautiful and slightly wrong.

## 04-capek.png
*The right word* (slide 8)

A theatre mask lying face-up and tilted: an oval face-shell with two cut-out eye holes and an
open oval mouth, a raised brow ridge, visible shell thickness at the curved edge. Beside it, one
brass cogwheel resting on its side, and a squat glass inkwell with a steel pen standing in it.
Nothing else. Cold accent: the ink. Mood: the props of a play about machines, after the
performance.

## 05-steam.png
*Four industrial revolutions* (slide 10)

A Victorian engineer crouched low in front of a beam engine's great flywheel and connecting rod,
long-spouted oil can in one hand, the other braced on the floor, cap pushed back, waistcoat and
rolled sleeves. The flywheel fills the upper right, far larger than he is. Cold accent: the
flywheel rim. Mood: absorbed, unbothered by the scale of the thing.

## 06-arm.png
*What a robot is today* (slide 11)

A six-axis industrial robot arm bolted to a heavy pedestal, articulated in a working posture,
a spot-welding gun at the wrist, cable looms strapped along the forearm, a small burst of sparks
where the gun touches a steel panel. Brass and riveted rather than modern plastic. Cold accent:
the steel panel. Mood: indifferent competence.

## 07-warehouse.png
*And by how it moves* (slide 12)

A low wheeled mobile robot — no legs, no humanoid form at all: a flat driven base on small
castored wheels carrying a stack of four stacked crates, with a short sensor mast at the front
holding a single lens. Riveted brass base plate. Cold accent: the lens. Mood: unglamorous and
entirely effective.

## 08-automaton.png
*Unmet expectations* (slide 17)

A full-size steampunk humanoid automaton mid-stride, chest open to show a ribcage of brass rods
and coiled springs, a round pressure gauge set into the sternum with a blank unmarked dial, a
wind-up key seated between the shoulder blades, articulated three-fingered hands. Confident,
almost theatrical walking posture. Cold accent: the gauge glass. Mood: the future as it was
advertised.

## 09-brain-gear.png
*2023* (slide 19)

A glass bell jar on a brass stand containing a brain built entirely from interlocking cogwheels
and small lenses. A brass cable runs out from under the jar's base to a disembodied mechanical
hand lying palm-up beside it — riveted palm plate, fingers built from three tapered segments with
visible pin joints, a two-band wrist collar where the cable enters. Cold accent: the glass.
Mood: intelligence with a body attached as an afterthought.

## 10-stage-mud.png
*2026* (slide 20)

Two identical brass automata. The first strikes a confident presenting pose on a small curtained
theatre stage. The second lies face-down and half-sunk in a churned muddy field in the
foreground, one arm still raised. Same machine, two worlds. Cold accent: the standing water in
the mud. Mood: quietly damning.

## 11-hand-word.png
*Skills now move between bodies* (slide 23) and *Back to the Golem* (slide 52)

A human hand entering from the left, cuffed in a workshop sleeve, offering a small flat clay
tablet bearing one incised angular notch — a geometric mark, not a character of any alphabet.
Facing it on the right, an open mechanical hand waiting palm-up: riveted palm plate, four
segmented fingers and a thumb built from tapered plates with pin joints, a two-band wrist collar.
The two hands do not touch; a clear gap between them. Cold accent: the human sleeve. Mood:
the instant before an instruction is handed over.

## 12-scale.png
*The research bench collapsed too* (slide 26)

A merchant's two-pan balance. On the left pan, a large brass automaton head. On the right pan, a
small untidy pile of blank coins. The coin pan has sunk to the bottom and the head pan has swung
high. Cold accent: the beam. Mood: the cheap thing outweighing the expensive one.

## 13-pocketwatch.png
*Bigger is not the answer on a robot* (slide 28)

A small open pocket watch lying in the foreground with its movement exposed and its blank dial
face turned aside, dwarfed by a towering cathedral clock mechanism of enormous interlocking gears
rising behind it. The tiny one is clearly the working one. No numerals anywhere. Cold accent: a
single large gear at the back. Mood: scale is not the point.

## 14-factory.png
*The best-evidenced deployment* (slide 34)

A human worker in overalls and a brass humanoid automaton standing side by side, each gripping
one end of the same wooden crate, lifting it together onto a roller conveyor at the right. Same
height, same posture, same effort. Cold accent: the conveyor rail. Mood: ordinary shift work.

## 15-lease-cart.png
*From CapEx to OpEx* (slide 41)

A heavy riveted automaton sitting upright and passive on a flat wooden handcart, hands resting on
its knees, being wheeled away by a man in a merchant's apron who leans into the handles. Cold
accent: the apron. Mood: the machine as stock, not as property.

## 16-fishing-net.png
*And the shortage is real* (slide 43)

A fisherman hauling a wide dripping net up out of the water. The net is full, but almost
everything in it is cogs, bolts and broken springs — only two or three actual fish among the
metal. Cold accent: the water. Mood: a big haul, very little of it worth keeping.

## 17-seal.png
*One date for your calendar* (slide 44)

A partly unrolled document, its surface completely blank with no writing or ruling of any kind,
weighted at one corner by a pair of brass calipers, with a large wax seal hanging from a ribbon
at its lower edge. The seal is stamped with the impression of a cogwheel. Cold accent: the
ribbon. Mood: bureaucracy arriving for the machines.

### If an image comes back wrong

- **Too clean / vector-looking** → add *"visible pencil construction lines left under the ink,
  brush edges slightly ragged, the wash bleeding a little past one contour"*.
- **Background not flat** → add *"the background is a single flat fill of #F7F4EE, absolutely
  uniform, as if the subject were drawn on a blank ivory page"*.
- **Text creeping in** (dials, seals, scrolls are the usual culprits) → add *"the dial has no
  markings at all; there is no writing anywhere in this image"*.
- **Drifting off-palette** → re-attach `01-golem.png` and say *"match this palette exactly"*.
